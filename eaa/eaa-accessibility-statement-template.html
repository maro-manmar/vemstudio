EAA accessibility statement: what to include, with an example

5-minute read · Updated November 2026

Under the European Accessibility Act, service providers must give customers information about how their service meets the accessibility requirements. For an online shop, the usual way is a dedicated page called “Accessibility” or “Accessibility statement”, linked from the footer. In Germany, the BFSG asks for this information in your terms and conditions or in another clearly visible way.

The seven parts of a good statement

  1. Your service. Shop name, domain, what you sell and which channels are covered (website, app).
  2. Standards. The EAA and your national law, plus your technical reference: EN 301 549 and WCAG 2.2 Level AA.
  3. How you meet the requirements. Concrete features: keyboard operation, text alternatives, contrast, labelled forms, captions.
  4. Compliance status. Fully or partially compliant, with an honest list of known limitations and alternatives.
  5. How you checked. Date and method: checklist, automated tools, manual keyboard and screen reader tests.
  6. Feedback and contact. Email and phone for accessibility problems, with a response time.
  7. Enforcement. The market surveillance authority customers can contact if they are not satisfied.

Short example

“[Shop name] aims to meet the accessibility requirements of the European Accessibility Act. Our technical reference is EN 301 549 and WCAG 2.2 Level AA. The shop is partially compliant: some older product videos do not yet have captions; the product details are available as text on every page and captions will be added by [date]. If you find a barrier, contact us at [email]. We reply within 5 working days.”

Common mistakes

  • Claiming “fully compliant” without testing.
  • Hiding the statement where nobody can find it.
  • No contact channel, or a contact form that is itself not accessible.
  • Never updating the date after changes to the shop.

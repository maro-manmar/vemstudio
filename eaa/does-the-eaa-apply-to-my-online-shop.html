Does the European Accessibility Act apply to my online shop?

4-minute read · Updated November 2026

The European Accessibility Act (EAA, Directive (EU) 2019/882) has applied since 28 June 2025. Since then, national authorities have moved from warnings to inquiries, and in some countries to fines and court cases. The first question every shop owner asks is simple: does this apply to me? Answer these four questions.

1. Do you sell to consumers in the EU?

The e-commerce rules of the EAA cover services offered to consumers. If you sell only to businesses (B2B), the e-commerce part does not apply. If even part of your sales goes to private customers in an EU country, keep reading.

2. Is the purchase completed online?

A website, a web shop on Shopify or WooCommerce, or an app where customers order and pay is an “e-commerce service” under the EAA. The whole buying journey counts: search, product pages, cart, checkout and account.

3. Are you bigger than a microenterprise?

Microenterprises providing services are exempt. A microenterprise has fewer than 10 people and no more than €2 million annual turnover or balance sheet total. If you have 10 or more employees, or exceed €2 million, the exemption does not apply.

4. Are you outside the EU?

Location does not protect you. A shop in the UK, the US, Switzerland or anywhere else that sells to consumers in the EU must meet the same requirements. The rules follow the customer.

If you answered yes to questions 1 and 2 and you are not a microenterprise, treat your shop as in scope. The practical target is WCAG 2.2 Level AA, the technical reference used with the harmonised standard EN 301 549.

What to do next

  1. Run a free automated test (WAVE or Lighthouse) on your home page, a product page and the checkout.
  2. Test the whole purchase with the keyboard only.
  3. Publish an accessibility statement and link it from your footer.
  4. Fix the most visible barriers first: contrast, focus, labels and alt texts.
